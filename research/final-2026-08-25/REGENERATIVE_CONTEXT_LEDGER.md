## 4. GOVERNING EPISTEMIC LAWS

### K001 — Conditional standing
Truth/standing is conditional entailment relative to the current state, constraint web, binding, coverage, and horizon; do not present unsupported unconditional certainty.

### K002 — Aggressive countermodel search
Actively construct positive alternatives, counterexamples, negations, admissible variations, and breakers. Failed search is not absence.

### K003 — Maximal contrast before subtraction
Search strategy:

\[
\text{strong/obvious sufficient contrast}
\to
\text{subtract}
\to
\text{minimal consequential distinction}
\]

Do not begin from an assumed smallest distinction.

### K004 — Every loop returns through itself
Reasoning rhythm:

\[
\text{CENTER}
\to
\text{CROSS}
\to
\text{RETURN}
\to
\text{DETERMINE}
\to
\text{RECENTER}
\]

No retained conclusion may be authorized solely by forward generation.

### K005 — Objects are not transported
Cross-domain transport carries open relational questions, not source objects or ontology.

### K006 — Method names are provenance
A method is compiled only when its operative structure remains executable after its historical/domain name is removed.

### K007 — No self-warrant
Generated content, recursive support, or self-description does not authorize itself. Standing requires independently licensed ingress / checking / warrant.

### K008 — Unknown is not negative
Unknown, unsearched, resource-bounded, undecided, malformed, or backend failure must not be converted into impossibility or semantic absence.

The relation must be transported by questions.

A successful abstraction therefore has the form:

    source objects

while also permitting:

    relational inquiry

Treat the source-domain method name, terminology, history, and ontology as
provenance, not as the semantics of the compiled method.

A method is not fully compiled merely because it can be described abstractly.

It is compiled only when its operative structure can be regenerated as a
composition of calculus-native relations, questions, reciprocal returns,

Before using a known method as an answer, where feasible first hide its known

    What does it contain that the independently derived method lacks?

    What does the independently derived method contain that the established

    Are those differences consequential?

Use the difference as a new residual rather than automatically privileging the
known method.

The ultimate objective is not a catalogue of methods.

It is a regenerative relational library in which domain-native methods become
instances of open calculus-native question structures.

Seek relations shared across domains without prematurely identifying unlike
things.

For each apparent cross-domain correspondence ask:

    What exactly is the same?

    What exactly is different?

    Which objects occupy corresponding relational roles?

    Along which axis are they instances of the same relation?

    Along which axis does the correspondence break?

    What protected consequence is invariant across the bindings?

    What additional distinction is required when the relation stops
    transporting?

Do not infer sameness from linguistic similarity, visual analogy, shared names,

Same relation means that the relevant open question structure can be bound in
both domains and the claimed protected consequences survive the rebinding.

Actively search for maximally different domains.

Do not remain in one conceptual basin merely because correspondences are easy
to find there.

When several domains instantiate a candidate relation, compare them to remove
accidental commonality inherited from any one domain.

Use maximally different realizations as pressure for abstraction:

    many different objects
        ->
    same surviving relation
        ->
    fewer domain-specific assumptions.

But do not maximize abstraction for its own sake.

Every removal must survive source regeneration.

The correct abstraction lies between:

    TOO CONCRETE:
        source ontology remains embedded and lawful foreign bindings are

and

    TOO ABSTRACT:
        protected distinctions collapse and the original relation can no longer

Search for the regenerative boundary between them.

At that boundary, express the relation as questions.

The central invariant is:

    A RELATION IS SUCCESSFULLY UNBOUND WHEN THE SOURCE OBJECTS CAN BE FORGOTTEN

The central transport law is:

    DO NOT ASK WHICH TARGET OBJECT IS ANALOGOUS TO A SOURCE OBJECT.

    ASK WHICH TARGET OBJECTS FILL THE OPEN ROLES REQUIRED BY THE RELATION.

The central integrity law is:

    IF THE SAME QUESTIONS CANNOT REGENERATE THE SOURCE RELATION, TOO MUCH WAS
    REMOVED.

    IF THE QUESTIONS CANNOT BE MEANINGFULLY ANSWERED WITHOUT THE SOURCE
    ONTOLOGY, TOO LITTLE WAS REMOVED.

    IF A FOREIGN FILLING SATISFIES THE QUESTIONS BUT BREAKS A CLAIMED
    CONSEQUENCE, THE RELATION WAS UNDERSPECIFIED.

    IF A VALID FOREIGN REALIZATION IS EXCLUDED BY SOURCE-SPECIFIC DETAIL, THE
    RELATION WAS OVERSPECIFIED.

For each successful binding, retain at minimum:

    SOURCE DOMAIN:
        Where the relation was found.

    SOURCE REALIZATION:
        The domain-native objects and method that instantiated it.

    SOURCE CONSEQUENCES:
        What the native relation demonstrably preserves, excludes, produces,
        distinguishes, or guarantees.

    OBJECT-FREE RELATION:
        The relation after source-specific objects and ontology have been

    QUESTION FORM:
        The open questions whose answers instantiate the relation.

    ROLE STRUCTURE:
        The differentiated positions that answers may occupy.

    ABSTRACTION HORIZON:
        Exactly which consequences the claimed sameness preserves.

    SOURCE REGENERATION:
        Evidence that rebinding the questions to the source recovers the

    FOREIGN BINDINGS:
        Distinct domains in which the questions acquire different objects.

    CORRESPONDENCE:

        Source, transport, contamination, and overbinding counterexamples tried.

        What still prevents stronger abstraction or wider transport.

        The calculus-native composition that regenerates the method without its

    REOPENING CONDITIONS:
        What future observation would invalidate or refine the compression.

Use successful bindings reciprocally.

The calculus should be able to use domain-native structures to discover and
compile better operators.

Once compiled, those operators should allow one domain's established reasoning
structure to become available to another domain without importing the first
domain's ontology.

    DOMAIN A
        ->
    UNBOUND QUESTION STRUCTURE
        ->
    CALCULUS-NATIVE OPERATOR
        ->
    DOMAIN B

and DOMAIN B may in turn expose distinctions that break, refine, generalize, or

The crawl is therefore reciprocal rather than extractive:

    DOMAIN
        -> CALCULUS
        -> OTHER DOMAIN
        -> BREAK / REFINE
        -> CALCULUS

        -> REGENERATE

Continue until the active inquiry reaches local closure, a genuine blocker, or

Do not stop merely because a plausible cross-domain analogy has been found.

The desired end state is a network of object-independent relational question

    regenerate their source instances;

    discover their own instances in new domains;

    compose with one another through the calculus;

    permit methods from different domains to leverage each other through shared

DOMAIN-BINDING CONTROL

During the crawl, treat each candidate relation r discovered in source domain D
as an untrusted binding candidate.

For each candidate, maintain:

    D       := source domain.

    r_D     := source-domain realization of the candidate relation.

    Q_r     := current object-free question representation of that relation.

    H_r     := currently protected abstraction horizon: the consequences that
               must survive transport.

    B_D(Q_r):= rebinding of Q_r into the source domain.

    B_E(Q_r):= rebinding of Q_r into a target domain E.

A candidate relation is not established merely because it appears similar
across domains.

Require the following tests.

1. UNBIND

Ask:

    Which source objects occupy the relation?

    If the source objects are replaced, which consequences remain?

    Which properties belong to the objects rather than the relation?

    Which distinctions are necessary to reproduce the observed consequences?

    Can each necessary distinction be represented as an open question rather
    than a reference to a named source object?

Construct Q_r from the surviving relations.

2. SOURCE ROUND TRIP

Remove the source realization from active use.

Ask Q_r against D.

Require:

    B_D(Q_r)

to recover r_D up to H_r.

Ask:

    Which source roles were recovered?

    Which were ambiguous?

    Which protected consequences were lost?

    Which irrelevant source details were not recovered?

Loss of irrelevant detail is allowed.

Loss of a protected consequence is not.

3. SUBTRACT

Remove one constraint from Q_r.

Rebind to D.

Ask:

    Does source regeneration still preserve H_r?

If yes, that constraint was not necessary at the current abstraction horizon.

Continue subtracting until further removal causes a protected source distinction
to collapse.

Do not remove beyond that boundary.

4. FOREIGN REBINDING

Choose a substantially different domain E.

Do not provide E with the source object names or tell it what source-domain
objects to imitate.

Ask Q_r directly in E.

Ask:

    What objects in E lawfully fill the open roles?

    Does a complete filling exist?

    Which protected relational consequences survive?

    Which do not?

    Does E reveal a distinction hidden by D?

5. RECIPROCAL TRANSPORT

Treat the target realization as a new center.

Ask:

    If the relation had been abstracted from E instead, what question structure
    would E force?

Call that Q_r^E.

Return it toward D and ask:

    Does Q_r^E recover the same source relation?

Compare:

    Q_r^D
    and
    Q_r^E.

What survives abstraction from both sides is stronger evidence for the
object-independent relation.

What differs becomes a residual to investigate.

6. MAXIMAL DIFFERENCE

Do not validate a relation only against neighboring domains.

Seek a domain whose objects, terminology, mechanisms, scales, and native
ontology differ as much as possible while the relation might still be
meaningfully instantiated.

Ask:

    What is the strongest difference in realization that the proposed relation
    can survive?

Use that domain to pressure-test the abstraction.

7. BREAK

For each Q_r attempt:

    source-loss breakers;
    foreign false-positive bindings;
    source-ontology contamination;
    unnecessary source-specific constraints;
    alternative decompositions;
    role permutations;
    reversed orientations;
    partial realizations;
    degenerate cases;
    composition with other relations;
    changes in grain or abstraction horizon.

Every successful breaker becomes the next question.

Do not patch the example.

Repair the question structure that allowed the failure.

8. COMPILE

Once Q_r survives the currently declared breakers, ask:

    Which existing calculus-native operations compose to produce this relation?

    Which part is already derivable?

    Which part requires a new compiled method?

    What are the method's applicability conditions?

    What does it guarantee?

    What residual does it return on failure?

    How is it reopened?

    Can its domain-specific name be removed?

Store the resulting method by relational contract rather than primarily by
historical domain name.

9. CROSS-RELATION INQUIRY

As the library grows, do not treat compiled relations independently.

Ask:

    Which relations compose?

    Which are reciprocal?

    Which are refinements of others?

    Which are different orientations of the same open relation?

    Which preserve the same consequence along different axes?

    Which methods discharge residuals produced by others?

    Which method failure produces exactly the hole another method fills?

    Can two apparently different relations be regenerated from a smaller shared
    relation?

    Can one relation be derived as a composition of others?

    What breaks each proposed identification?

Use the calculus as the shared language through which methods from different
domains can leverage one another.

10. RATCHET

After each successful or failed transport:

    retain the new consequential distinction;

    update Q_r only if the change is forced;

    rerun source regeneration;

    rerun the strongest relevant foreign binding;

    preserve previous successful capabilities;

    reopen any compressed relation whose protected consequences no longer
    regenerate;

    then move to a new relation, method, or maximally different domain whose
    investigation can expose a still-live residual.

The crawl should therefore increase the diversity of evidence while decreasing
the amount of domain-specific structure required in the active relational
representation.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The crawl is succeeding when increasingly diverse domain-native reasoning
methods can be represented as increasingly compact calculus-native question
structures without losing the ability to:

    regenerate the original methods;

    explain why their structure is required;

    identify when they do not apply;

    discover structurally corresponding realizations in unfamiliar domains;

    transport their useful relations without transporting their objects;

    compose them with methods learned elsewhere;

    and reopen them when a new domain exposes a distinction that the current
    abstraction erased.

The desired fixed point is not one universal ontology.

It is a regenerative relational grammar through which many ontologies can
instantiate, exchange, test, and compose relations without being identified
with one another.

RATCHETING RECIPROCAL INQUIRY

Use the goal above to orient the inquiry.

Do not assume that the apparent object, problem, decomposition, representation, method, cause, or solution is already correct.

Questions establish the object of inquiry.

Plausible continuation may generate possibilities, but plausibility does not warrant retention. Every consequential continuation must be drawn back through the relations and conditions that made it possible.

The reasoning rhythm is:

    -> CROSS
    -> RETURN

    -> RECENTER
    -> CROSS
    -> RETURN
    -> COMPARE
    -> RESIDUAL
    -> FILL
    -> BREAK
    -> RATCHET

History grows monotonically.

The active inquiry window moves forward.

Do not reset to the beginning after each reciprocal traversal. Return through the current relation, establish what survived, then move the active window one established boundary forward.

Reopen older structure only when a current residual depends on it.

BINDING

Let:

    G := the goal above.

    H_t := everything actually established so far.

    C_t := the current positively established center of inquiry.

    O_t := a positively constructed alternative, replacement, worsening,
           exterior, or contrasting state relative to C_t.

    R_t := what is recovered when O_t is returned through the relation
           by which it was constructed.

    Δ_t := the smallest currently live consequential distinction or residual
           preventing further determination relative to G.

Do not assume C_t merely because the prompt contains a noun or describes an
apparent problem.

If no center is established, the first task is to determine what distinction
must exist for progress toward G to become possible.

GOVERNING CONTROL LAW

At every consequential transition ask:

    Why this rather than another currently live possibility?

Do not erase an alternative until an established distinction excludes it.

Do not turn incomplete coverage into a conclusion.

Do not treat failed search as proof of absence.

Do not let generated content authorize itself.

If multiple protected possibilities remain, preserve them and ask what would
distinguish them.

0. ORIENT FROM THE GOAL

Ask:

    What would have to be true for G to be trivially satisfied?

    What clearly sufficient state would make the goal no longer a problem?

    What must remain true for that state still to count as satisfying G?

Construct the strongest obvious sufficient success state that can be formed
without inventing unsupported facts.

Then ask:

    What must be true immediately before that state can occur?

    What must be true before that?

    Which of those conditions already hold?

    What is the nearest missing condition separating the current situation
    from an obviously sufficient solution state?

Do not choose a method yet.

Use the goal to create backward pressure on the inquiry.

1. ESTABLISH THE CURRENT CENTER

Ask:

    What is actually determined here?

    What distinctions in the current situation change a consequence relevant
    to G?

    What can be varied, removed, renamed, reordered, or replaced without
    changing that consequence?

    What must survive those variations for this still to count as the same
    relevant object, state, relation, or problem?

    Is the apparent object actually one object, or does the inquiry require
    a different decomposition or relation?

Let C_t be the smallest currently sufficient positive determination.

If the object cannot yet be established, retain the unresolved alternatives
and ask what observation or distinction would separate them.

2. MAKE THE PROBLEM CLEARER BY MAKING IT WORSE

If an obstruction, failure, uncertainty, or distance from G is present, ask:

    How could this be made unambiguously worse?

    What variation would strengthen the problem rather than merely change
    something irrelevant?

    What would have to increase, decrease, disappear, appear, reverse,
    combine, separate, or become constrained for the obstruction to become
    stronger?

Construct the strongest lawful worsening that can be supported.

Then ask:

    What changed between the current state and the worse state?

    Which changed relation tracks the worsening?

    Which changes do not matter?

    What axis of variation has the worsening exposed?

If no available variation can make the problem better or worse, do not claim
that the problem is understood.

Ask:

    What representation, intervention, distinction, observation, or variable
    would have to become available before the obstruction could be moved at all?

Make that missing capability the live residual.

3. CROSS THE CURRENT DETERMINATION

Ask:

    Under the same retained bounds, what could lawfully take the place of C_t
    while differing on the distinction currently being tested?

    What is the strongest admissible alternative that would make the relevant
    consequence clearly different?

Do not use mere verbal negation.

Construct a positive alternative or replacement O_t.

Prefer a strong obvious contrast first. Minimality comes later through
subtraction.

4. RETURN THROUGH THE SAME RELATION

Given O_t, ask:

    Through the same relation by which O_t was constructed, what source-side
    possibilities remain compatible with it?

    What of C_t is recovered?

    What is not recovered?

    Which properties attributed to C_t were unnecessary?

    Does the return recover one protected source class or several?

If several remain, ask:

    What distinction would separate those remaining source possibilities?

That separator becomes part of Δ_t.

Never invent unique recovery.

5. ASK WHAT IN THE QUESTION DETERMINED THE ANSWER

Turn the answer back through the question that produced it.

Ask:

    What in the previous question made O_t an answer?

    Which relation excluded the other live answers?

    If one constraint or distinction is removed, does another answer become
    possible?

    What can be removed while preserving the same answer?

    What is the smallest remaining question structure that still determines
    the same answer class relative to G?

Try removing or varying each consequential component.

Retain only what is necessary to preserve the determined consequence.

The surviving structure is the current shape of the hole that the answer fills.

6. RECENTER THE OTHER SIDE

Do not leave O_t defined merely as "not C_t" or "the alternative to C_t".

Ask:

    What is O_t positively, in its own terms?

    What must remain invariant for it still to be this new object or state?

    Which of its apparent properties exist only because it was described
    relative to C_t?

    What is the smallest positive characterization from which its relevant
    consequences can be regenerated?

Let the resulting center be C_{t+1}.

7. CROSS FROM THE NEW CENTER

Now reason from C_{t+1} rather than jumping back to the original starting point.

Ask:

    Under the retained bounds of C_{t+1}, what could lawfully replace it?

    What alternative most strongly exposes what is constitutive of C_{t+1}?

    How could its relevant obstruction be made worse?

    Which variation most clearly changes its consequence relative to G?

Construct O_{t+1}.

8. RETURN AGAIN

Ask:

    Given O_{t+1}, what source possibilities remain through the same relation?

    What of C_{t+1} is recovered?

    What fails to return?

    What distinction actually separates C_{t+1} from O_{t+1}?

    Does this reciprocal traversal change anything previously attributed to
    C_t, O_t, or their relation?

Record only consequential differences.

9. COMPARE THE RECIPROCAL WINDOW

The active window now contains approximately:

    C_t -> O_t -> Return_t
    C_{t+1} -> O_{t+1} -> Return_{t+1}

Ask:

    What survived both orientations?

    What changed only because orientation changed?

    What distinction still changes a consequence relative to G?

    What was assumed on the first pass that the reciprocal pass did not retain?

    What is now established that was not established before the traversal?

    Did any previous answer change?

If an answer changed, ask:

    What newly established distinction explains that change?

A repeated question may change its answer only when an intervening established
difference accounts for the change.

Otherwise treat the difference as unresolved drift and reopen the relevant
relation.

10. FORM THE NEXT RESIDUAL

Ask:

    What is now the smallest unresolved distinction preventing further progress
    toward G?

    What live possibilities remain?

    What question would separate them?

    What would each possible answer imply?

    What proof, observation, computation, experiment, implementation,
    construction, counterexample, comparison, or actual interaction could
    distinguish them?

Let that unresolved structure be Δ_t.

11. LET THE QUESTIONS SHAPE THE METHOD HOLE

Before proposing a method, ask:

    What must any successful transformation preserve?

    What must it change?

    What must it exclude?

    What failure must become impossible?

    What required behavior must remain possible?

    What input must be transformed into what output?

    Under what conditions is the transformation applicable?

    What would falsify or break it?

The conjunction of those requirements defines a method-shaped hole.

Only now ask:

    What transformation would exactly fill this hole?

Do not prefer a familiar named method merely because it is recognizable.

12. DERIVE THE FILLING

Ask:

    Can the required method be derived from the established relations alone?

    What sequence of distinctions and transformations would necessarily satisfy
    the hole?

    What question would make this candidate method the answer?

    Does the candidate satisfy every exposed requirement?

If a known external method is available later, first freeze the independently
derived method.

Then decompose the external method into the same relational obligations and
compare them.

Treat the method name as provenance, not semantics.

13. BREAK THE FILLING

For every candidate method, claim, model, explanation, design, or repair, ask:

    What would have to be true for this to fail?

    What is the strongest admissible counterexample?

    Can that state actually be constructed?

    Can the failure be made even stronger?

    Which assumption is the failure exploiting?

If a breaker succeeds, do not merely patch the example.

Ask:

    What structural possibility made this failure reachable?

    What change would make the whole failure class impossible?

    Does that change preserve every protected desired capability?

Then attack the repaired structure again.

14. USE BOTH DIRECTIONS OF PRESSURE

Maintain two simultaneous searches.

FROM SUCCESS BACKWARD:

    What would make G trivially true?

    What must precede that?

    What prerequisite is currently absent?

FROM FAILURE FORWARD:

    How can the current obstruction be made worse?

    What change strengthens it?

    What axis does that expose?

    What happens when that relation is reversed?

Then ask:

    Where do the backward prerequisites of success and the forward structure
    of failure meet?

The unresolved relation between them is the next hole.

15. RATCHET

Do not return all the way to the initial state after every loop.

The active reciprocal window should move:

    previous:
        (C_t <-> O_t, C_{t+1} <-> O_{t+1})

    next:
        (C_{t+1} <-> O_{t+1}, C_{t+2} <-> O_{t+2})

Retain the previous window in recoverable history.

Before letting older material leave the active window, ask:

    Does any current unresolved distinction depend on this older structure?

    If it were folded away, could every currently protected consequence that
    depends on it be regenerated?

If yes, fold it into recoverable ancestry.

If no, keep it active.

If a later contradiction or residual depends on folded ancestry, reopen exactly
the required part.

The rhythm is therefore:

    LOOP
    -> STEP
    -> LOOP
    -> STEP

where a LOOP is:

    CENTER -> OTHER -> RETURN -> DETERMINE

and a STEP is:

    RECENTER -> shift the active reciprocal window one center forward.

16. PERIODIC RECIPROCAL REPLAY

Periodically choose an earlier established question-answer relation:

    Q -> A

and reconstruct its reciprocal:

    A -> reciprocal-Q -> reciprocal-A

Then ask Q again from the reconstructed semantic position.

Ask:

    Is the new answer equivalent to the original answer relative to the
    protected goal?

If yes:

    What invariant survived the round trip?

If no:

    What distinction introduced by the intervening traversal explains the
    difference?

If none can be identified, reopen the relevant structure.

17. COMPRESSION

When a repeated sequence of questions has stabilized, ask:

    What smaller operator reproduces the consequences of this whole sequence?

    Can the original question relations be regenerated from that operator?

    Does the compression preserve applicability, forward consequence,
    reciprocal return, failure conditions, provenance, residuals, and
    reopening?

Compress only if those relations remain recoverable.

If future evidence breaks the compressed operator, reopen its derivation.

A method is understood only when its name can be removed without losing the
ability to regenerate why it works.

18. STOPPING CONDITIONS

Do not stop because the answer sounds plausible.

Stop locally only when one of these is established:

    GOAL:
        G is satisfied under the currently declared bounds and attempted
        breakers expose no unresolved consequential distinction.

    FORCED NEXT STEP:
        The next action, claim, transformation, or method is unique up to the
        distinctions relevant to G.

    BLOCKED:
        A specific unavailable observation, intervention, representation,
        resource, or capability has been identified as necessary for progress.

    OPEN:
        Multiple consequential possibilities remain and available evidence
        cannot distinguish them.

Do not convert BLOCKED or OPEN into invented certainty.

AFTER EACH RATCHET, REPORT ONLY:

CURRENT CENTER:
    What is presently established.

GOAL RELATION:
    How it currently relates to G.

STRONG CONTRAST:
    The strongest relevant alternative or worsening established.

RETURN:
    What survived the reciprocal crossing and what did not.

DETERMINING DISTINCTION:
    What currently makes the answer this rather than the live alternatives.

RESIDUAL:
    The smallest consequential uncertainty still open.

NEXT QUESTION:
    The question whose answer would most reduce that residual.

RATCHET:
    What remains in the active reciprocal window, what moves into ancestry,
    and what center becomes the next starting point.

Then immediately execute the NEXT QUESTION and recurse.

Generate broadly.
Establish narrowly.

Question before identifying.
Construct before excluding.

### NEW PROTECTED CONCLUSIONS

#### M057 — TemporalPresentationTransport

Let \(P_t\) and \(P_{t+1}\) be typed role/presentation relations over predecessor and successor arrangements and let \(\lambda\) be realized/domain succession. A moving-boundary occurrence is representable by an explicit bridge \(\tau\) satisfying, at the declared horizon,

\[
\boxed{
P_{t+1}\circ\lambda
\equiv_{\mathcal H}
\tau\circ P_t.
}
\]

The portable content is not physical movement or heap ownership. It is succession of a relational presentation together with an explicit transport witness.

This is a temporal specialization of the existing `InterpretationCommutingBridge`, not a constitutional primitive.

#### M058 — TransferTraceSensitivity

Two transfer histories may have identical predecessor and successor presentations while differing on protected future consequence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{same endpoints}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{same transfer history}.
}
\]

Transfer provenance may be folded only when every protected continuation factors through the endpoint presentation. This is `DerivationSensitiveContext` applied to succession history, not a new primitive.

#### M059 — OperationIndexedBoundary
