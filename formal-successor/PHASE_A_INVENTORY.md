# Phase A predecessor inventory

This surface makes omissions visible before successor definitions are selected. It is an
overinclusive, generated view of pinned predecessor sources, not a semantic interpretation of
them and not evidence that Gate A has passed.

## Authority separation

- TeX declarations, narrative candidates, list items, and mathematical displays are extracted
  from the byte-pinned v2.0 semantic authority.
- Rust modules and visibility lines are predecessor implementation evidence. Their presence does
  not turn them into calculus primitives or correct the TeX.
- fixture files, migrations, conformance rows, and executable-evidence registry entries are
  schema or conformance evidence.
- structural TeX lines that do not form candidate items still participate in the ordered source
  coverage digest, so an extractor cannot silently remove them from the admitted universe.

The exact source classes, identity rules, and Gate A law are data in
`PREDECESSOR_INVENTORY_GRAMMAR.json`. `PREDECESSOR_INVENTORY.json` is its deterministic generated
projection over the canonical TeX and predecessor commit named by the grammar.

## Stable identity and contraction

Content candidates use their class plus normalized content digest; exact duplicate content gains
a deterministic occurrence suffix. Whole repository files use pinned paths, while public Rust
surfaces additionally bind path, kind, name or fallback line identity, and source digest.
Line numbers are source loci, never the sole identity.

The initial field is deliberately wider than the final formal predecessor surface. It may be
contracted only by assigning an explicit reviewed destination or disposition. Deleting a candidate
because it looks expository, redundant, implemented, or inconvenient is not classification.

## Checks

```text
node tools/predecessor_inventory.js check
node tools/predecessor_inventory_check.js
```

The first command requires exact regeneration. The second independently re-reads the pinned
sources and rejects missing declarations, list items, repository blobs, Rust visibility lines,
fixture-row occurrences, altered source ranges, duplicated identities, detached coverage, and Gate
A self-promotion. Fixture labels admit slash-delimited phase components and one optional terminal
variant letter; repeated displayed labels retain separate source occurrences rather than collapsing
through a label-keyed map.

Generation and green checks establish the extraction/source-coverage boundary only. Gate A stays
`PENDING` while any consequential candidate remains pending review, any executable fixture lacks
its required claim edge, or an admitted source form is unmatched.

The TeX-local contraction of this overinclusive field is governed by
`PHASE_A_TEX_CLASSIFICATION.md`. Its overlay preserves these generated identities while separating
explicit declarations, binding results, unresolved claim boundaries, and canonical context.

The Rust semantic/public and schema contraction is governed by
`PHASE_A_IMPLEMENTATION_CLASSIFICATION.md`. Its exact-symbol edges are correspondence candidates,
never semantic authority or Phase F preservation results.

The corrected fixture-row field and its execution relations are governed by
`PHASE_A_FIXTURE_CLASSIFICATION.md`. The corrected pinned boundary contains 190 status-row
occurrences with 185 displayed labels; all 19 registry labels have status rows.
